## Rules for writing questions and solutions

Use short, natural situations with a clear person, object and question. Every expression must arise from the story. Avoid extra “assume x is between…” sentences. Those old bounds were a generation check that made geometric dimensions positive; they were not needed in the student’s algebra task. Internal physical-validity checks may remain. Keep new repeated-group contexts free of exponents.

For every Lesson 2-5 solution, write the question first, list one given quantity per row, then explain the operations. Do not compress several givens into one sentence. Type 212 illustrates six rows: tables, packs per table, selling price per pack, one donation, making cost per pack and one supplies expense. Keep quantities, units, repetitions and one-time amounts consistent between the story and solution.

Use Parentheses, Circle, Like Terms only when those steps are needed. After expanding, circle every term with its sign, including constants and terms that do not match another term. Combine full terms, such as −2x−5x=−7x; do not introduce factoring as a shortcut for combining. A recognition or translation question needs a direct explanation. A distribution-only task can stop once the resulting terms are unlike.

For factoring, retain neutral method names and the teacher’s division of every term. A negative-one extraction, when shown, follows the positive divisions in a separate final step. Do not append an unrequested distribution check or force a particular factor sign when another permitted factored form is mathematically correct.

Difficulty is fixed per type. Each active type has ten distinct flat variants, A–J. Keep four to eight types per lesson/star group. The available pool is not a requirement to assign every problem: a pool of 30 or 40 three-star questions is reasonable. Current Lesson 2-1 has four three-star types, therefore 40 available variants.
